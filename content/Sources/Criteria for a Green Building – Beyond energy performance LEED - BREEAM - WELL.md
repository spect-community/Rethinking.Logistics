Lecture 16 of the *Rethinking.Logistics* expert lecture series at ARCHIP, 05.5.2026.

## Lecture announcement

In the presentation, we will present the reasons why green buildings make sense for both investors and building users, and of course for the surrounding nature.

A green building includes a very complex set of aspects that are defined by certification systems and are therefore comparable and controllable. A green building in the sense of certifications such as LEED or BREEAM goes much further than the energy efficiency that is commonly addressed today.

This is only one piece in the mosaic of credits that define the green standard of a quality building. A certified building then guarantees the owner and user that they have a quality building that supports good conditions for work and rest, and that the building is designed and will be operated efficiently and economically.

This can also be presented on specific examples of buildings in the Czech Republic.

*From the announcement of the lecture series (LinkedIn, #RethinkingLogistics).*

## About this source

Certification is only marginally about energy — the money case rests on people, because energy is ~1% of a company's budget while sick days, productivity and rent premiums are not. Zahradník walks the ČSOB South Headquarters (LEED v3, 90 credits) as a best case, and concedes in Q&A that where the investor is not the end user the game becomes hunting the cheapest credits, and that whole credit blocks are decided by the site, not the design. Křečková then shows the same gap at room scale: Czech daylight code has no upper limit, so a floor plate can be fully legal and still have a quarter of its area unusable from glare and overheating.
