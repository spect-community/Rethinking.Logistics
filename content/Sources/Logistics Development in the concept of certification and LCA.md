Lecture 14 of the *Rethinking.Logistics* expert lecture series at ARCHIP, 28.4.2026.

## Lecture announcement

This lecture explores the topic of logistics construction through the lens of certification methodologies, which today fundamentally influence how buildings are designed and evaluated. The presentation will introduce SBTool as a sustainability assessment tool, detailing its structure and the weighting principles of its individual criteria. Additionally, it will highlight RBTool, which focuses on building resistance to climate change impacts and the concept of resilience in architecture. In a broader context, the lecture demonstrates how these approaches are transforming our thinking regarding logistics facilities, from their environmental impacts to their ability to withstand future conditions.

The follow-up lecture will focus on calculating the carbon footprint of buildings as a key tool for assessing their environmental impact. It will introduce primary data sources, the tools currently in use, and the latest requirements for these evaluations. Special attention will be paid to why calculation results may vary and how to interpret these differences correctly. In a broader context, the lecture will demonstrate how to work effectively with Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) results during both the design phase and the formal assessment of buildings.

## About this source

Two UCEEB researchers dissect what certification systems (LEED, BREEAM, DGNB, SBTool CZ) actually measure, where they misfire on logistics halls — buildings with almost no users, enormous footprints and territorial impact — and argue that resilience is a missing layer on top of sustainability. The second half is a hands-on account of whole-life carbon: EU legislative deadlines, why LCA databases and their metadata determine everything downstream, and case studies where the "obvious" low-carbon choice (timber, reuse, recycled aggregate, tree offsetting) fails to show up in the numbers.
