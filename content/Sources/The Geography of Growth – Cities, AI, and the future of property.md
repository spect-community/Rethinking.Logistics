Lecture 13 of the *Rethinking.Logistics* expert lecture series at ARCHIP, 24.4.2026.

## Lecture announcement

In this session Oxford Economics will present an overview of the role cities and regions play in driving national economic activity. The presentation will outline why place matters for growth, the key structural factors driving economies, and the key themes shaping our future outlook. The role of technological change, AI, frontier sectors, productivity, and demographics will all be covered, and a particular focus on what it means for real estate will be outlined.

After graduating from the University of Warwick with an MSc in Economics, Dan joined Oxford Economics’ Cities & Regions Forecasting team. Over the past six years, he has worked extensively across the team’s European and Global services, gaining significant experience in building subnational forecasting models, producing timely written analysis, and presenting insights to clients across sectors including real estate, asset management, financial services, and local government. Dan also leads Oxford Economics’ technical and climate scenario services for Europe and has delivered a wide range of bespoke consultancy projects in this area.

## About this source

The meaningful unit of economic analysis is not the country but the city: a small set of European urban economies do the growth heavy-lifting, and their trajectories are being reset by four forces — demographic concentration, a weakening industrial base under high energy costs, a structural housing undersupply, and an AI/data-centre buildout whose real constraint is electricity and land, not demand. Europe's share of global city GDP is falling (~33% in 2000 → ~20% in 2025 → ~15% forecast 2050), and where new digital infrastructure lands will be decided by renewable energy access, grid capacity and physical climate risk.
