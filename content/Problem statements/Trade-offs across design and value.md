## The problem

Clear height raises storage volume and rent but also structure, embodied carbon and urban scale. Green and public land improves biodiversity and acceptance but reduces leasable area ([[Sustainability Unboxed – Delivering on Logistics and Distribution Warehouses]], [[Ilora.ai – Industrial Real Estate Analytics (glossary)]]).

- [[Clear height]] ↔ [[Embodied carbon intensity]], [[Structural utilisation]], [[Visual impact on neighbours]]: see [[Building up against carbon and energy]].
- [[Net operating income per m²]] ↔ [[Green area ratio]], [[Publicly accessible area]], [[Land take]]: see [[Space for people and nature against storage]].
- [[Green rent premium]] ↔ [[Construction cost]], [[CapEx–OpEx ratio]]: see [[Upfront cost against running cost]].
- [[Daylight autonomy]] ↔ [[Thermal comfort]] (translucent roof panels: less lighting energy, more overheating): see [[Daylight against overheating]].

## What the evidence says

- [[A taller shed stores half as much again]] — Per the Ilora glossary, an 11 m (36 ft) clear height stores about 50% more pallets than 7.3 m (24 ft) on the same footprint.
- [[Height buys efficiency and buys a fire problem]] — Going taller improves automation efficiency but triggers in-rack sprinklers, ventilation and exhaust systems. Fire suppression is "super expensive", so there is a trade-off.
- [[Over-build the height, don't charge for it]] — When a tenant asks for 8 m clear height, Panattoni builds 10 m and absorbs the cost, because constraining height forecloses future uses.
- [[Translucent roofs trade lighting carbon for heat]] — Roof lights cut lighting energy and possibly embodied carbon but raise indoor heat in hot climates, so the metrics must be weighed together.
