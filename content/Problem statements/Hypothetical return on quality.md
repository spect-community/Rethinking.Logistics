## The problem

For new buildings, the return on sustainability appears only through higher rent, lower vacancy or cheaper capital. For retrofits it can be immediate ([[Sustainability Unboxed – Delivering on Logistics and Distribution Warehouses]]).

A rent premium is captured by whoever owns the building over time. For a developer who sells on completion it only matters if it raises the sale price.

## What the evidence says

- [[Retrofit pays now, new-build pays hypothetically]] — Efficiency and renewables in existing sheds can pay back immediately for owner and tenant; in new-build the return appears only through rent, vacancy or cost of capital.
- [[No green premium in logistics yet, but it is coming]] — An investor reports no pricing premium or discount for logistics performance yet, slower than in other sectors, though one is expected.
- [[90% of Dutch occupiers would pay more for green space]] — In the Netherlands most industrial occupiers accept a rent premium for sustainable space, driven mainly by compliance needs.
- [[Certified sheds valued 25 bps higher]] — BREEAM-certified logistics assets in the UK and Europe were valued about 25 bps above non-certified ones and let faster.
- [[IPUT parks let at about 10% above a typical unit]] — IPUT's Dublin parks achieve a rental premium of about 10 % above a typical warehouse unit.
- [[BREEAM Outstanding added 4.82% to capital cost]] — The capital cost uplift for BREEAM Outstanding on a large UK warehouse was +4.82 %, and under 0.5 % for Very Good or Excellent.
- [[Resilient assets, resilient returns]] — Investors back green buildings when the return is visible; resilience is argued as return stability.
- [[Refinancing now comes with climate-risk questions]] — Lenders ask more about environmental and flood risk at refinancing, so climate risk is now part of financial operations.
- [[Energy performance is a financing instrument]] — Every project carries an EPC that must be shown to banks and investors; landing below a threshold value buys better financing terms.
- [[Certificates are a language for banks]] — Certification's real function is as a common language linking investors, tenants and banks. Banks tie loan volume to rating level: gold unlocks the full sum, silver a smaller one.
- [[The real green lever is 0.1–0.2 points of interest]] — One incentive that can move developers is financial: banks with deeper ESG policies can offer 0.1 to 0.2 better interest rate, which at these capital volumes is significant. Banks underwrite over 30–40 years, a longer horizon than the investor's — which is why the alignment works.
- [[Sustainability as risk elimination]] — Working with Amazon taught the company that their "unique approach to sustainable operation" is inseparable from being risk-free.
- [[Old sheds are becoming unlettable]] — European logistics is bifurcating: over 80% of take-up in Italy and the UK is now in modern stock, over 80% of vacant space in the Netherlands is second-hand, and UK assets built before the 2000s have seen negative net absorption since the pandemic. Older stock struggles to meet modern requirements and "retrofitting is often impractical due to structural limitations."
