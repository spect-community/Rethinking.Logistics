## The problem

Owners often lack whole-building operational data, because tenants control meters and bills ([[Sustainability Unboxed – Delivering on Logistics and Distribution Warehouses]]). The industry report itself identifies the missing link as an information gap in implementation, measurement and benchmarking.

## What the evidence says

- [[Energy data collected like rent]] — Leading owners treat energy data and energy certificates as a core asset KPI, collected with the same routine as rent and occupancy.
- [[Logistics was GRESB's lowest-scoring sector]] — In the 2023 GRESB survey logistics participated less and scored lowest on average, with fewer certifications than office or retail.
- [[The real energy breakthrough is building management data]] — Asked about technological breakthroughs, he names building management systems first: developers and landlords are getting good at collecting performance data and tuning buildings to time of day and season, with AI as the next step. A colleague adds that the missing half is knowledge about the people inside.
- [[Rethink the normalising unit per typology]] — kgCO2/m² is the default but not always useful: carbon per patient for a hospital, per m² or per tonne of storage for a warehouse, per MWh or per MW of capacity for a data centre.
- [[Lighting is 65–95% of a dry warehouse's energy]] — In non-refrigerated warehouses lighting dominates energy use, and LED retrofits cut lighting electricity costs by up to 70%.
