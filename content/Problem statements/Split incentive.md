## The problem

Under triple-net leases the tenant pays energy and operations. Efficiency savings rarely reach the owner's operating expense ratio. They reach the owner only indirectly, through rent, occupancy, valuation and financing terms ([[Sustainability Unboxed – Delivering on Logistics and Distribution Warehouses]]).

The same split hides the largest cost of a logistics operation. If labour costs several times more than rent, a building that helps attract and keep staff can pay back its extra cost through the operator, even when the developer does not see the benefit. This is a hypothesis to test.

## What the evidence says

- [[Under triple-net, the savings walk out with the tenant]] — Owners decide on construction but tenants pay utilities, so efficiency investments rarely return to the owner directly.
- [[Energy is 1% of a company's budget]] — For a typical office, retail or shopping-centre operator, energy costs are around 1% of the overall company budget, which is why owners are not strongly motivated by energy-efficiency measures; the people-related metrics move the budget far more.
- [[Transport is half the supply-chain cost, rent a tenth]] — For a standard ambient distribution warehouse, roughly 50% of supply-chain cost is transport, 20–30% labour, and only 5–10% rent. This inverts the usual real-estate framing.
- [[Labour costs five to seven times the rent]] — Rent is about 5 % of supply chain costs; labour costs $5–7 for every $1 of rent.
- [[Logistics job vacancies doubled in five years]] — The European logistics job vacancy rate doubled to almost 3 % in five years; Czechia 5.6 %.
- [[Workplace investment helps keep warehouse staff]] — Workplace investment helps attract and retain warehouse employees.
- [[Lease length is the hidden design brief]] — Logistics tenants sign 5–7 year leases; manufacturers sign 15, and increasingly 20–25 years, because relocation costs are prohibitive.
- [[Ownership is the tenant's defence against the landlord's ratchet]] — Built-to-own warehouses are invisible in the lease data everyone reports, yet would, he expects, correlate strongly with manufacturing: a tenant who invests heavily in automation cannot afford to be re-priced at renewal, so buys to control the real estate and the location.
