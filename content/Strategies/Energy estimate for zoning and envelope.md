## The question it answers

How much energy does a smaller heated zone or a better envelope save?

## How it works

1. Estimate the baseline: a benchmark intensity times floor area, or a monthly energy simulation of the existing or typical building.
2. Model the design move, such as a smaller actively heated zone, insulation or an adaptive façade.
3. Compare annual or monthly demand, split into heating, cooling, lighting and technology.
4. Keep the move if the saving is significant, and size PV or other supply to the remaining demand.

## In the student projects

- [[Elsa Hauksdottir]] — *manual / formula*. [[Heating energy demand]]: baseline 600,000 kWh/yr vs proposal 360,000 kWh/yr. Decision: “The energy estimate supported adaptive thermal zoning with manual movable partitions and foldable racks, so the active heated zone shrinks off-peak and the dormant area goes to public use.”
- [[Vladi Kostecki]] — *an energy simulation tool that is not named*. [[Energy use intensity]]: original envelope 48,000 kWh/month vs improved envelope 30,000 kWh/month, approx. 37.5% annual reduction. Decision: “Upgrade the envelope with insulation and sun-responsive façade panels (approx. 37.5% less energy).”
- [[Sara Gani]] — *an energy simulation tool that is not named*. [[Energy use intensity]]: before 220–280 kWh/m²a; after 110–160 kWh/m²a.
- [[Lindsay Daphne Macuja]] — *Grasshopper energy model (probably Honeybee, inferred from the graphics)*. [[Energy use intensity]]: monthly peaks approx. 75,000 kWh in January/December. Decision: “The energy profile supported putting PV on the sawtooth roof.”

Values and decisions as reported by the student; see each case study for the page in the portfolio.

## Where it falls short

- In [[Elsa Hauksdottir]]'s project the benchmark intensity is not cited, and two pages give different proposal values (360,000 and 300,000 kWh/yr).
- Two of four projects do not name the simulation tool.
