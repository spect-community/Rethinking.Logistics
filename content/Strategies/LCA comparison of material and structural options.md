## The question it answers

Which materials or structural system give the same building the lowest embodied carbon?

## How it works

1. Model the structure and envelope, or the existing building, as a bill of materials.
2. Run a whole-life LCA (product stages A1–A3 and, where possible, the later life-cycle stages) for a baseline and for one or more alternatives.
3. Read the result per m² and by life-cycle stage or resource type to see where the carbon sits.
4. Choose the option with the lower embodied carbon, and check side effects such as cost or biodiversity stress.

## In the student projects

- [[Lindsay Daphne Macuja]] — *One Click LCA*. Embodied carbon intensity: baseline 420 → proposal 354 kgCO2e/m² (Carbon Class B); [[Social cost of carbon]] 258,618 €. Decision: “The One Click LCA comparison supported choosing low-carbon concrete with 25% GGBS and 97% recycled steel reinforcement (420 → 354 kgCO2e/m², class B).”
- [[Ece Yasar]] — *One Click LCA, with its Biodiversity Stress module*. LCA of the existing cross-dock across stages A1–A5, with concrete, HVAC components and plastic membranes as the largest contributors; [[Biodiversity stress from materials]]: 7,920 PDF/m²/yr from materials.
- [[Sara Gani]] — *a web LCA tool (probably One Click LCA, inferred from the graphics)*. Embodied carbon: current materials 800, proposed materials 520; [[Life-cycle impact by stage]]: A1–A3 materials dominate (GWP approx. 70%).

Values and decisions as reported by the student; see each case study for the page in the portfolio.

## Where it falls short

- [[Aycha Chambashala]] names One Click LCA as the tool for comparing timber, steel and concrete, but shows no results.
- In [[Sara Gani]]'s project the calculation basis of the 800 → 520 comparison is not described.
- Only one project ([[Lindsay Daphne Macuja]]) shows a baseline-versus-proposal LCA that directly decided a material; [[Ece Yasar]] assesses the existing building only.
