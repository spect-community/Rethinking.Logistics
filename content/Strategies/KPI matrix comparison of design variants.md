## The question it answers

Which of the design variants performs best across all the aims at once?

## How it works

1. Develop two or more variants of the concept.
2. Choose the KPIs that matter for the brief, often from different domains (carbon, greenery, traffic, social).
3. Score or calculate each variant on each KPI and put them in one matrix or chart.
4. Develop the variant that scores best overall, or rule out variants that fail one KPI.

## In the student projects

- [[Sara Gani]] — *manual / formula*. KPI matrix of three variants. Decision: “The L-shaped layout that groups offices, control rooms and café/shop in one wing and storage/docks in the other was kept because it scored best across the KPI matrix while separating truck, car and pedestrian flows.”
- [[Julia Kopác]] — *manual / formula*. KPI comparison with a typical warehouse, e.g. [[Impervious surface ratio]] typical 47% vs proposal 4.9%. Decision: “The KPI comparison was used to justify placing modular warehouses over the highway … The carbon KPI ruled out the underground-road alternative.”
- [[Aycha Chambashala]] — *manual / formula*. two alternatives compared on RVPI, environmental performance and internal traffic circulation. Decision: “Comparing the two alternatives on RVPI, environmental performance and internal traffic circulation led to terraced U- and L-shaped volumes set back from the edges, with separate truck and car circulation and an added entrance.”
- [[Ece Yasar]] — *manual / formula*. KPI comparison of the existing state and the proposal: 0% green roof, no wildlife connection, all roof runoff to drainage. Decision: “The KPI comparison (0% green roof, no wildlife connection, all roof runoff to drainage) supported turning the existing roof into a planted platform and extending it as a green bridge across the roads to reconnect the two forest patches.”
- [[Marek Nový]] — *manual / formula*. KPI comparison of the scheme. Decision: “The KPI comparison was used to justify the layered layout (warehouse, workshop and community zones) … No variant-level comparison is shown, so the evidence supports the scheme as a whole rather than a specific choice.”

Values and decisions as reported by the student; see each case study for the page in the portfolio.

## Where it falls short

- Most matrices compare the proposal with the existing state or a typical building, not several design variants.
- The case studies do not say how the KPIs were weighted, that is, how a better score on one aim was traded against a worse score on another; see [[Trade-offs]].
