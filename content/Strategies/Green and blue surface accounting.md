## The question it answers

How much of the site is green, permeable and able to hold rainwater, before and after the design?

## How it works

1. Measure the sealed, permeable, green and green-roof areas of the existing site.
2. Measure the same areas in the proposal, and add retention volumes such as green roofs and cisterns.
3. Express them as ratios of the site or roof area, so they can be compared with a target.
4. Use the target to decide how much ground to free, for example by stacking the programme or building over infrastructure.

## In the student projects

- [[Adam Večeřa]] — *manual / formula*. [[Impervious surface ratio]]: before 90% sealed → ≥60% permeable; [[Stormwater retention volume]]: current ~30 m³ → 800 m³. Decision: “The KPI targets (permeable ≥60%, public space 45%, tree canopy 62%, ecological corridor E–W) supported stacking the IKEA program vertically to free ground for a public park, pond and an east–west ecological corridor.”
- [[Julia Kopác]] — *manual / formula*. [[Impervious surface ratio]]: typical 47% vs proposal 4.9%; [[Green area ratio]]: typical warehouse 55,278 m² vs proposal 127,553 m². Decision: “The KPI comparison was used to justify placing modular warehouses over the highway with an arch-truss hybrid structure, freeing ground for continuous greenery and pedestrian links.”
- [[Ece Yasar]] — *manual / formula*. [[Green roof coverage]]: existing 0% → proposal 50% of the roof platform planted. Decision: “The KPI comparison … supported turning the existing roof into a planted platform.”
- [[Vladi Kostecki]] — *manual / formula*. [[Green area ratio]]: original 0 m²; new 34,000 m². Decision: “…turn the brownfield into a zoned public park.”
- [[Khrystyna Verbitska]] — *manual / formula*. [[Green area ratio]]: approx. 210 m² of new green surface on a 600 m² site, approx. +35% vegetated area. Decision: “Reuse an abandoned building … which supports the embodied carbon saving and the green and public space gains.”

Values and decisions as reported by the student; see each case study for the page in the portfolio.

## Where it falls short

- The projects measure green area in different ways (absolute m², ratio of site, ratio of roof), so the results are not comparable.
- None of these five projects uses a weighted method such as the [[Biotope area factor]] for this comparison.
