## The conflict

A compact box minimises façade, cost and heat loss and keeps internal circulation simple. Splitting it into smaller volumes improves views, daylight, human scale and passage for wildlife, but adds façade, cost and embodied carbon and can leave unused space between blocks.

## In the student projects

- [[Adéla Berkovitz]] — Splitting the mass into separate buildings is less financially efficient (cost) but gives human scale, better neighbourhood integration and passage for small animals (biodiversity / visual impact).
- [[Aycha Chambashala]] — Alternative 1 (two large blocks) gave clear visual and operational movement but scored low on residential view preservation and environmental performance (tunnel effect, 'wall' to neighbours).
- [[Aycha Chambashala]] — Alternative 2 (dispersed blocks) gave variety but lowered internal traffic circulation efficiency and created unused 'dead zones' between masses.
- [[Lindsay Daphne Macuja]] — Breaking the warehouse into smaller human-scale modules improves daylight and safety but increases façade area by about 30%, raising embodied carbon and construction cost (questionnaire).
- [[Natalie Žižková]] — Not stated explicitly; the geometry optimiser balances façade perimeter (cost, heat loss) against the fixed usable-area target of 5,000 m².
