## The conflict

Every square metre given to green or public space, and every window in a racked wall, is taken from storage. Layouts that separate flows or open the site to residents tend to use more land and store less.

## In the student projects

- [[Sara Gani]] — More windows and open areas raise daylight comfort for workers but take wall space from shelving, reducing storage capacity (daylight comfort vs. storage capacity).
- [[Sara Gani]] — Splitting the programme into two separate buildings improved traffic separation but used too much site area and isolated workers (traffic separation vs. space utilisation / worker cohesion).
- [[Sara Gani]] — The campus layout gave more public space for residents but lowered storage and was inefficient for truck circulation (accessible green/public space vs. storage capacity and logistics efficiency).
- [[Vladi Kostecki]] — More green space on the property means less storage space (green space area vs. storage capacity) (questionnaire).
- [[Roshni Basu]] — Green space preservation (% kept green) competes with the footprint of inserted hub units and market functions.
- [[Sara Sulollari]] — Green planted area competes with usable public space; resolved by combining planting with social courtyards (questionnaire).
