## The conflict

Docks, yards and linear processing flows take space and need durable paving. This reduces permeable area and can block ecological connections, so the projects move ecology to the perimeter, route water to planted channels or add green infrastructure elsewhere.

Green area is also easy to count and hard to connect: a share of the site can be met with leftover strips that do not form a continuous habitat (see the common misreading in [[Habitat loss and cut ecological corridors]]).

## In the student projects

- [[Ece Yasar]] — Improved truck flow and dock capacity (logistics efficiency) reduces permeable area (stormwater), which the student proposes to mitigate with added green infrastructure (questionnaire).
- [[Ece Yasar]] — Material biodiversity stress from the building (7,920 PDF/m²/yr) is set against on-site habitat creation, but the offset is only claimed qualitatively.
- [[Ilinca Maria Baciu]] — Heavy truck areas must stay hard and durable, limiting the permeable surface ratio; water there is routed to planted channels and retention instead.
- [[Adéla Berkovitz]] — A linear logistics flow (processing efficiency, less forklift travel) blocks a bio-corridor through the plot, so ecological elements move to the perimeter (questionnaire).
- [[Adéla Berkovitz]] — Green roof coverage conflicts with hazard safety: the hazardous storage building cannot have a green roof.
- [[Robin Jesenský]] — Reducing cooling water use and cooling operating cost brings no biodiversity or ecological benefit to the site (questionnaire).
