## The conflict

Lighting is the largest energy use in a dry warehouse, so daylight through the roof is an obvious saving. The same roof lights let in solar heat, which raises indoor temperatures in hot climates; the two metrics have to be weighed together.

## What experts and publications say

- [[Translucent roofs trade lighting carbon for heat]] — Roof lights cut lighting energy and possibly embodied carbon but raise indoor heat in hot climates, so the metrics must be weighed together.
- [[Lighting is 65–95% of a dry warehouse's energy]] — In non-refrigerated warehouses lighting dominates energy use, and LED retrofits cut lighting electricity costs by up to 70%.
