## The conflict

Planted roofs and façades are a common way to give a logistics building ecological value. Their extra load has to be carried by the structure, which raises construction cost and embodied carbon, especially on large single-storey spans.

## In the student projects

- [[Adam Večeřa]] — Adding green facades and roof (green roof/wall coverage, biodiversity) increases structural weight and therefore initial cost (CapEx) and may affect building operation (questionnaire).
- [[Elsa Hauksdottir]] — Maximising green roof coverage conflicts with minimising structural cost and embodied carbon (questionnaire).

As reported by the student; see each case study for the page in the portfolio.

## What experts say

- [[Green roofs are a biodiversity investment, not a carbon one]] — They don't help with carbon, and on a single-storey large-span logistics roof the added mass would massively increase structure. They make sense where column centres are narrow or the building is already multi-storey. The reframing: if you double the structure to carry the roof, what habitat was destroyed to win that material?
- [[Green roofs vs LCA — an admitted conflict]] — Putting nature on a roof adds substantial structural load, which worsens the carbon calculation; she names this as a genuine conflict.
