## The conflict

Putting storage, parking or hubs underground keeps the ground free for green space and limits visual impact. Excavation and retaining structures are carbon- and cost-intensive, so the projects weigh hidden volume against a lighter above-ground scheme.

## In the student projects

- [[Adam Večeřa]] — Going vertical frees ~120,000 m² of ground for park and ecology but requires deep underground storage and parking (−24 m), which conflicts with the embodied-carbon strategy 'no underground / avoid deep foundations' listed under KPI 2.3.
- [[Katrin Rybinskiy]] — Building underground would maximise green space and hide the volume but needs energy-intensive retaining walls, raising embodied CO2 and cost; building above ground is lower carbon but obstructs residents' views (questionnaire).
- [[Roshni Basu]] — The courtyard/historic-centre concept gives deeper integration and social value but higher embodied carbon and CapEx because of underground construction, while the panelák concept is cheaper and has higher solar share (40–70% vs 20–40%) (questionnaire).
