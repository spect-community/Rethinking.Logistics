## The conflict

Conveyors, robotics and automated partitions make operations faster and less labour-intensive. They raise electricity use and operating cost, and a building tailored to one automated process may be hard to reuse.

## In the student projects

- [[Hynek Hrabík]] — Adding conveyor belts and automation (needed for cargo continuity under the bays) raises energy consumption, conflicting with operational energy and carbon (questionnaire).
- [[Julia Kopác]] — Higher automation level (lower labour cost) increases electricity use, i.e. operating energy demand (questionnaire).
- [[Elsa Hauksdottir]] — Robotic partitions reconfigure much faster (5–15 min vs 2–4 h) and need less labour, but cost more to operate and have higher environmental impact than manual partitions.

As reported by the student; see each case study for the page in the portfolio.

## What experts say

- [[Tailoring is a one-way door]] — Buildings can be tailor-made "to the point where it's not usable for the future", and with Amazon or H&M investing multiples of the building's value in technology, the developer has no choice. Since the chance that another tenant uses such a building the same way after ~20 years is very limited, it must be possible to recycle it easily, ideally on site.
- [[80% of the market need, not 100% of one tenant]] — The investor horizon is 15–20 years while leases run 5–10; a building must fit roughly 80% of market demand so tenants can be swapped without touching the asset's fundamentals.
