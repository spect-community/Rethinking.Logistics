## The conflict

Large column-free halls, buildings over infrastructure and rooftop uses all ask more of the structure. Every gain in span or load has to be paid for in material, which shows up as embodied carbon and construction cost.

## In the student projects

- [[Adéla Berkovitz]] — Rooftop car parking would save land but needs heavier columns for the larger loads (structural material/embodied carbon), so parking stays on the ground (consultation).
- [[Robin Jesenský]] — A thicker copper shell (10 mm vs 5 mm) doubles copper mass and its embodied GWP, a choice the student lists without resolving.
- [[Julia Kopác]] — Cost to build roughly doubles (600 to 1,220 EUR/m²) in exchange for large gains in uninterrupted greenery, walkability, noise reduction and lower sealed-surface share.
- [[Julia Kopác]] — Choosing a vertical, above-ground scheme over an underground road option was driven by the embodied-carbon KPI, at the expense of a more complex long-span structure.

## What experts say

- [[The biggest possible hall reflex is a carbon cost]] — Longer spans mean bigger beams; accepting shorter spans shrinks the structure. The level of flexibility built into a logistics building has to be balanced against it.
