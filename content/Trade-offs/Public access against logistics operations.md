## The conflict

Public programmes, courtyards and open sites make logistics part of the city. Visitors and residents meet trucks, dispatch peaks and round-the-clock operation, so the projects separate uses by floor or zone, keep units compact or accept higher maintenance.

## In the student projects

- [[Khrystyna Verbitska]] — Mixing public programmes with logistics increases community space and social integration but creates operational conflicts, congestion and noise at peak dispatch times (public accessibility vs. operational efficiency); addressed by comparing a hybrid layout with a floor-separated layout.
- [[Eva Kažukolovaitė]] — Bringing public functions to the hub may add occasional noise and foot traffic for residents (social integration vs. residential comfort) (questionnaire 4.4).
- [[Thomas Olsen]] — Opening the site to the public and adding ecological features raises social value and biodiversity but may increase maintenance, wildlife disturbance and conflicts with logistics operations (public accessibility vs. operational efficiency) (questionnaire 4.4).
- [[Ilinca Maria Baciu]] — Adding public and green layers improves urban integration and biodiversity but lowers logistics efficiency and raises construction cost (questionnaire; also listed as unintended consequences).
- [[Roshni Basu]] — Social and ecological activation of courtyards (social interaction, green space activation) conflicts with logistics presence, which brings noise, visual impact and perceived intrusion; mitigated by keeping units compact and partly underground (questionnaire).
- [[Sara Sulollari]] — Public uses on the waterfront may conflict with port logistics operations and could lead to gentrification (questionnaire).
- [[Lindsay Daphne Macuja]] — More building edges and public-facing modules improve street activity but increase maintenance and long-term operational cost (questionnaire).

As reported by the student; see each case study for the page in the portfolio.

## What experts say

- [[Residential and logistics cannot share a block; offices and retail can]] — Mixed use is rejected for housing on operational grounds — logistics runs 24/7 — but offices, retail and light industrial co-exist well; one Prague business park dedicates a third of its zone to retail, showrooms and a supermarket serving the surrounding community. The binding limit on industry inside cities remains road access.
