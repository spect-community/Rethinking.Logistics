## The conflict

A roof can carry photovoltaics or planting, and a site can be open to the sun or shaded by trees. Projects that push renewable generation lose green and blue area, and projects that push canopy and green roofs lose solar access; some combine the two in a biosolar roof.

## In the student projects

- [[Katrin Rybinskiy]] — Using the roof of data centre C for PV raises renewable energy generation but leaves no roof area for green roofs, so green and blue area must come from the ground plane.
- [[Thomas Olsen]] — Maximising green roof area improves stormwater retention, biodiversity and thermal performance but reduces roof area for PV and therefore renewable energy production (green roof coverage vs. solar energy yield); resolved with a combined biosolar roof (questionnaire).
- [[Julia Godard Lombard]] — Maximising solar panels improves energy supply but adds structural load and lowers biodiversity net gain (questionnaire).
- [[Marek Nový]] — Implied by the KPI table, not discussed by the student: solar exposure falls from 92% to 77% as tree count (46 → 257) and shaded public space (8% → 23%) rise, so more shade and canopy cost sun access (and possibly PV/daylight potential).
