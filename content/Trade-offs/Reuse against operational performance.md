## The conflict

Keeping a building avoids the embodied carbon of new construction. Upgrading it adds cost and embodied carbon of its own, and a reused building that runs less efficiently may lose over its whole life, particularly for energy-intensive uses such as data centres.

## In the student projects

- [[Vladi Kostecki]] — Reuse with an upgraded envelope and adaptive façade raises upfront cost and embodied carbon of the renovation but lowers operational energy and running costs (CapEx/embodied carbon vs. operational energy) (questionnaire 4.4).

## What experts say

- [[Reuse can lose on whole-life carbon]] — Reusing an existing building saves upfront embodied carbon but often can't reach modern operational performance; on a whole-life basis the reuse can come out *higher*. That flips again if the energy supply is renewable enough.
- [[Don't force the wrong second life on a building]] — For data centres he would not compromise operational efficiency to reuse a shell: a 10%-less-efficient data centre likely loses over its life. His preferred move is relocation, not demolition — put the data centre elsewhere and give the existing building a use it suits.
- [[In a data centre the embodied carbon is in the pipes]] — For a typical office, a bit more than half of embodied carbon sits in sub- and superstructure. For a data centre the building fabric is "not that big of a concern" — the mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems dominate, which means the levers sit with equipment manufacturers, not just architects.
