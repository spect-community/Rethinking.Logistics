## What it measures

Whether the different uses of a mixed-use logistics development are tied together by a shared, connected green space, rather than just sitting next to each other. Companion to [[Mixed-use ratio]]: that one measures how much mix there is, this one measures whether nature holds the mix together.

## Calculation

1. Define the shared green spine or core (continuous, publicly accessible green space).
2. Map entrances (or active ground-floor frontage) of every use — logistics, offices, housing, retail, hotel, community.
3. Measure walking distance from each entrance to the spine.
4. Result = entrances (or frontage length) within the threshold distance ÷ total. Report per use type as well, to check that no use is left out.

Can be done in Grasshopper with shortest-path walking distances on the pedestrian network.

## Source

Franziska Meisel (Henning Larsen), lecture *Nature positive by design*, Q&A on the Sugar Valley master plan (former Siemens site, Munich): "a green spine in the middle that really is supporting all the different function. There's a hotel, housing, offices, very mixed-use, but it's really all bound to this kind of green space in the middle."
