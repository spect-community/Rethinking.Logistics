## What it measures

The extra rent a logistics building achieves because of its sustainability, design quality or amenities, compared with a comparable standard unit in the same market. How much more occupiers pay for certified or high-performance space.

## Calculation

(Rent of certified or high-performance unit − rent of comparable conventional unit) ÷ rent of conventional unit × 100, for comparable location, size and age.

## Reference values

Netherlands (Savills survey, cited by ULI): 90 % of industrial occupiers are willing to pay a premium, 70 % accept about 5 %, a quarter accept more than 10 %. No strong premium is evident yet in some markets (e.g. India).

- About 10 % above a typical warehouse unit at IPUT's Dublin parks, including [[Nexus Logistics Park]] (developer-reported, see [[IPUT – PERE interview with Niall Gaffney]]).

## Who captures it

A rent premium is captured by whoever owns the building over time. For a developer who sells on completion it only matters if it raises the sale price. This is why the metric is central to [[Designing for an unknown tenant]].
