## Context

Paul Astle (Ramboll) shows where carbon sits in buildings: in a 2020 Ramboll breakdown for an office, structure was about 60% of upfront carbon, and a study across residential, education and commercial buildings showed floors dominating the structure. He argues logistics will be similar because of its large slabs and roofs. Answering a student in the Q&A, he adds that in multi-storey buildings the efficiency of the floors drives the carbon.

## From the transcript

> We can see that about 60% is the structure […] as you can see in this study, it is again dominated by floors, really, really significant. And this is covering three different typologies, residential, education, commercial. Now I know that's not logistics, but it's likely to be very similar and the reason for that is that in logistics you are often having very large horizontal elements. So you have either a lot of ground floor slab or you have a large roof or you have multi Storey, but it's large horizontal elements and basically [embodied] carbon is driven by horizontal elements because that's where most of the mass is and that's where most of the carbon is. […] they should start with their floors and make sure their floors are as lean as they can possibly be, because that is where we can make big savings. — (Q&A) So if you only have one story, then the ground floor and the roof is probably the most significant. But when you have a multi-storey building, the efficiency of your floors is what drives the carbon in that building.
