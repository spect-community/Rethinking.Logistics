## Context

Julie Železná (UCEEB, CTU Prague) explains the two kinds of LCA data, specific data from Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs) and generic data, and when each should be used in design. The 90th-percentile example comes a few minutes later, in the metadata of a dataset in the Czech database.

## From the transcript

> You can have generic data and you can have specific data. […] specific data are mostly coming from EPDs. […] these are the most stable or like the best data you can get if you are really working with specific products. But sometimes […] you don't know what products you are going to use in your building, which is logical […] You don't know where you will get the product from. But actually there are quite large differences. […] there are really, really large differences between the producers. It depends on their technologies, what fuels they use. Again, it's connected to the electricity mix. And so for that case we have generic data. Generic data represent like a generally known material, like concrete, [bricks], so not a specific product. […] you take actually the most specific data you have and you somehow create a generic data of it, you aggregate it somehow, you make a percentile of the number of carbon footprint you get. And that's the generic data you should use at the beginning of your design actually, until you really know what products you will have in the building.
