A student asked which early signs Oxford Economics looks for when forecasting, given how quickly AI is changing. Parker explains what they would consider when forecasting AI's impact on an economy.

## From the transcript

> We don't actually at the moment formally forecast growth in AI because of data reliability. But […] if we were to forecast the impacts, for example, of AI on an economy, some of the things we'd consider would be […] the sector reliance in certain segments of the economy that are likely to benefit or utilize AI more. And what that could mean in terms of displacement of workers. […] Another area we would consider would be existing skills of the workforce. So we would look at things like education levels within cities and regions. We would look at existing time series on […] workers in the labour market and their willingness to return to education. So this links into this idea of upskilling of the labour force […] We'd also look […] at investment rates within cities. So […] data that we have on investment flows, investment in research and development, which would give us a better sense of which cities are most likely to adopt and utilize AI.
