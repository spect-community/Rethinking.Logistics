## Context

A student asked what matters most when designing regenerative logistics infrastructure today so that it can later be converted to other uses without difficulty, as Copenhagen's harbour was. Signe Kongebro (Ramboll) answers under the heading of flexibility, at urban and at building scale.

## From the transcript

> I think you're touching upon a very important thing and you could put this topic under the umbrella of flexibility. […] on a building scale […] on the hardware side, of course it's good that you have systems that can be changed […] but I would say the most robust strategy is […] both time and space. So the space you offer in the building can be used for different things. It can both become a school, it can become apartments, it can become offices. So the space offers so much quality that it can be used for more than one thing, not at the same time, but within a duration time of some time span. The other one was time, and time is how do we use the space during day. […] people are going home from school and then all the school buildings are empty the rest of the evening.
