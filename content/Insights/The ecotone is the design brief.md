## Context

Signe Kongebro's (Ramboll) closing thought, answering a student's question on whether large logistics hubs still have a place in the city centre. She links it to her last slide on places where systems meet, such as Tokyo Station, which mixes a train station with the city.

## From the transcript

> This is where two systems or three or four systems meet each other. As soon as a lot of systems are meeting, I would say you have an opportunity as a designer. This is the most complex area, but you need to not clean it up, but if you have a train station meeting a school, you need to make a station with a school. […] I hope we will see more new [typologies] where existing systems are meeting new program, and you have to insist on that friction. Otherwise I would say it's not resilient and sustainable. As an example, I can say if you're working with nature or landscape, you have what is called an ecotone. This is where the forest meet[s] the beach. There is a zone in the middle where it's not a forest and it's not a beach, but it's like a wetland. And that area has […] a higher biodiversity than the forest in itself and the beach in itself.
