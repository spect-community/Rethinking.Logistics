## Context

Signe Kongebro (Ramboll) presents a study made with Ramboll's chief economist on the societal and economic value of placemaking. She lays out its model: what designers build ('hardware'), the economic return, and the 'place feeling' that the hardware creates.

## From the transcript

> We know that the hardware, when we do design, it is about architecture, design, facilities, materials and so on. That is sort of the hardware we design. We also know that the hardware creates an economic return. That's why we have investors and developers and they put money into this and they get a return. But we also know that the hardware creates a place feeling. […] It's a good area. It's a nice neighborhood. We don't know exactly what is cooking. […] But what we do know is that the placemaking is actually where the money is made. This is where the societal money is made for more local shopping, stronger community and public life, more jobs and so on. But we also know that the result of good placemaking flips back to the economic return. So if you are [a] developer, if you have done a very good neighborhood area, you also know that your economic return of the hardware of the different buildings will rise.
