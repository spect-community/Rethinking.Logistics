## Context

A student asked whether, once a developer has brought infrastructure to a site, it makes sense to keep building around it. Pavel Sovička (Panattoni CZ & SK) agrees in principle, then explains why the infrastructure left on Czech brownfields often cannot be used.

## From the transcript

> […] the brownfield died and the activity stopped in 2002. That was a lot of cases where we are looking at the brownfields we have developed. You come there in 2012 to develop, but there were other parts of the city developing and utilising the infrastructure. So when we apply for the permit on the brownfield and we say, OK, we will have only 500 cars coming to the site instead of [in the past], let's say, 2000 cars when there was a really heavy industry, you have to go through the test on the junctions and you have to measure the current flow, but the current flow intensified because of the other developments. So you are told there's a 0 capacity on the junction which is at your brownfield, because simply there are cars passing by to other parts of the city. So you are in the situation that you have to upgrade the junction or do a new roundabout, but sometimes you are blocked because simply there's no space for it […]
