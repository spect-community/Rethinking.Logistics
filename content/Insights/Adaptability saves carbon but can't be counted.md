## Context

In the Q&A after the LCA part, a student noted that logistics centres have long been adaptable and asked whether adaptability, being able to reuse the building, matters more in LCA than material choice. Julie Železná (UCEEB, CTU Prague) answers that it has a large impact but the standard does not currently allow it to be modelled. The lifespan in her brick-industry example reads "103 years" in the transcript.

## From the transcript

> […] one thing is that you don't see probably this scenario in your LCA calculations. You can model it […] If you have the adaptable space, you will probably have longer service life of the building. You will not demolish it. You will just renovate or something, maybe fit out. But regarding the standard, this is not allowed now. […] when you don't rebuild the building, you don't demolish and build a new one, then you save a lot of carbon footprint. So yes, adaptability has a large impact on it, but […] I don't think that Europe has the way how to calculate it correctly, because you can also use it as green washing. You can say, OK, but my building is so good that it will last for [103, probably 100] years, which is for example the case of [Porotherm] or Wienerberger […] But it's not about service life [of] the material. It's about the […] moral service life of the building, because you demolish it not only because it's in not good state, but you demolish it because you don't like it anymore. You don't need this space. So if you make it adaptable, then […] you higher the probability that the next user will still use it.
