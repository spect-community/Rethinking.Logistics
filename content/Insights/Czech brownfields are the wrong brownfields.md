## Context

A student asked whether abandoned buildings in the city could be renovated for logistics, data centres, retail or last-mile hubs with a mixed-use programme. Pavel Sovička (Panattoni CZ & SK) says yes in principle, but explains that the Czech stock of old industrial buildings differs from Western Europe's.

## From the transcript

> Oh, sure. The, I would say, specific thing in the Czech Republic [and] Prague […] Ostrava is that you don't find any industrial stock, industrial buildings which were built, for example, in Germany, France, Spain in 80s, 90s. They simply don't exist as a light industrial in [Czech]. Most of the properties which were developed now are brownfields, old steel mills, heavy factories. Really, it's a heavy industry. And those are very difficult to convert to the, I would say, modern use for logistics, industrial or data centre. Some of those are kept and used as, let's say, venues for concerts […] because they were mainly heavy brownfields and polluted, you can't turn it [at], I would say, decent costs into residential, because to clean it up from all the pollution it will not make sense. So most of those are going down and there's a new development.
