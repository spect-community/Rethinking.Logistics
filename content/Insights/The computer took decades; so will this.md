## Context

After his answer on forecasting, Parker (Oxford Economics) was asked whether AI is a bubble. He calls his own answer 'sitting on the fence'.

## From the transcript

> The evidence we have so far suggests that in a lot of sectors [it] will be quite complementary. It won't be destructive. And also […] you'll see a lot of headlines at the moment talking about AI in the sense of it will lead to […] rapid job losses and also massive productivity enhancements. What we've seen throughout history is that with the invention of the computer, for example, it actually takes quite a long while, it takes often decades for these technologies to really be effectively seeped into cities and economies. So it's unlikely that we're going to see immediately […] a massive surge in productivity and job losses. […] there's still quite a wide gap in Europe between the north and the South. And that's kind of reinforcing existing trends that we're seeing, essentially with the rich northern cities compared to the relatively poorer southern cities. […] I think we need to be careful in terms of making sensationalist opinions on how AI is going to completely disrupt labour markets and we're all going to be out of the job in a year's time.
