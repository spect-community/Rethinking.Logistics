## Context

Paul Astle, decarbonisation lead at Ramboll Buildings, opens the context part of his lecture by breaking down the often-quoted share of global CO2 emissions attributed to buildings. He splits it into operational carbon of existing buildings and embodied carbon of new construction.

> […] buildings globally are responsible for about 37% of CO2 emissions, which is obviously quite a lot. […] you can see that's a huge number, 10 gigatons per year. When it comes to new buildings, that's where embodied carbon starts to come into play, and of embodied carbon, which is about 3.5 gigatons per year, the vast majority is associated with three materials, which is concrete, aluminium and steel.

## What needs checking

A verification pass against public sources (claim bank, fact-check item 1) found:

**13.5 Gt is wrong.** 37% is UNEP/GlobalABC's 2022-data figure for energy- and process-related CO2, totalling **≈9.8 Gt**; the 2024/25 edition revised the share to **34%**. Materials: 2.5 Gt from cement/steel/aluminium, ~3.7 Gt including bricks and glass — so "~3.5 Gt embodied" holds only with glass and brick included. [GlobalABC/UNEP GSR](https://globalabc.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/global_status_report_buildings_construction_2023.pdf) · [GSR 2024/25](https://www.unep.org/resources/report/global-status-report-buildings-and-construction-20242025)
