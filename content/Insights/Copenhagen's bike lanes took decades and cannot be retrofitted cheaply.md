## Context

A student asked whether cities plan infrastructure specifically for last-mile vehicles such as delivery robots and cargo bikes, which ride partly on roads and partly on sidewalks. Wiebke Müller (EIT Urban Mobility), based in Copenhagen, answers that it depends on each city's existing infrastructure and willingness to invest, using Copenhagen's bike lanes as the example.

## From the transcript

> How much do they want to invest into the infrastructure itself to make it adapted to the certain type of new innovations? Do they, for example, want to widen the bike lanes? Because otherwise the cargo bikes can't smoothly operate on it. For example, Copenhagen has these very wide bike lanes. They also have a small height difference so that people who bike there are actually feeling more protected. But of course, this is an infrastructure system which was built over decades. And there's nothing which can be easily with the low cost, I would say, be retrofitted, but it is for sure on a smaller scale included in the plans how to make the last mile […] more sustainable.
