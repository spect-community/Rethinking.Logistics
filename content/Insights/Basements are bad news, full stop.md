## Context

A student working on large data centres asked Paul Astle (Ramboll) whether building them underground, to take up less land and help biodiversity, would be better for the environment. Astle says basements are carbon-intensive and probably no help for biodiversity, unless going underground cuts operational carbon or an existing hole can be reused. Asked in a follow-up whether 300 parking spaces should go in a basement or on the ground, he says even an above-ground multi-storey car park is lower carbon.

## From the transcript

> So in general terms, basements are bad news for carbon and putting anything in the ground is extremely carbon intensive. […] So in general, we try and steer people away from basements if we can. Now the data centre thing is a slightly unusual situation because if you can reduce the operational carbon as a result of going in the ground some way, then potentially it might make more sense to do that. But I don't think it would be better from a biodiversity point of view, is my feeling. […] Yeah, if there's already a hole in the ground that you can make use of, it doesn't require huge amounts of carbon to resist lateral loads, then maybe makes more sense. […] Even if it's a multi Storey car park, it'll be low[er] carbon than going underground.
