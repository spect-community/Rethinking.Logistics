## Context

Pavel Sovička (Panattoni CZ & SK) describes Panattoni's brownfield redevelopments in Czechia and says the main difficulty is not pollution but missing infrastructure. He explains the reasoning with which the company first went into brownfields, and what happened to that infrastructure while the sites stood empty.

## From the transcript

> […] the difficulty we are finding in the brownfield development, rather than the environmental pollution, which is still of course […], is the missing infrastructure. Yeah, because our logic when we went into the brownfields was: when there was a steel mill with 30 megawatts of the power brought to the site and there was going 1500 trucks a day, [there were] working 6000 people. That's infrastructure, right? But the [Czech] zoning and master plans don't grant you the availability of the infrastructure. So what was happening is that the city was developing around, it was consuming the capacity of the road infrastructure, sewage plant, electricity, and the cities were left with the brownfield which doesn't have capacity, or the roads around to carry any traffic, not the former traffic, but any traffic.
