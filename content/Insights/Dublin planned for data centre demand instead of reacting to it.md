## Context

Martins (Ramboll) contrasts standalone data centres in open land with planning cities with data centres as part of the equation, using an example from Dublin.

## From the transcript

> So data centres have been very much developed organically, you know, projects have been, you know, jumping on plots of land here and there. What I like about this example, which is taken from Dublin, is the fact that as a city was planning its energy systems based on where they want development to happen in the future, where they want large infrastructure, social infrastructure such as hospitals, schools, housing development, offices, etc. They integrated the demand for data centres in a way to say, we want them in a particular part of our city […] as opposed to how it happened in Dublin prior to this […] whatever piece of land developers could find, they would essentially go and buy it and develop a data centre. So the tool of planning, of urban planning, really saying to the market, we want data centres in particular places is a fundamental one. And it's a tool that is unfortunately not used enough around the world and around Europe in particular.
