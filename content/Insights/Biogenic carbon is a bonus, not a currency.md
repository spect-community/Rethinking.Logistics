## Context

In the materials part of his presentation, Paul Astle (Ramboll) explains how the carbon stored in timber shows up as a negative figure on an environmental product declaration (EPD), and why it should not be netted against fossil emissions.

## From the transcript

> […] typically a kilogram of dried timber contains about half a kilogram of pure carbon, which in turn is the equivalent of 1.8 kilogram of biogenic CO2. […] it should never be combined with fossil carbon, so if we combine that biogenic carbon with the emissions associated with the material, we can end up with a negative figure, and that's fundamentally wrong. The reason it's wrong is that it can mask efficiencies or inefficiencies and poor design. […] And I've worked on projects 15 years ago where people were talking about using more timber. So basically using timber really inefficiently because the figure was more negative. […] ultimately I would say treat biogenic carbon as a bonus. […] but we shouldn't be using it to offset the carbon in our fossil emissions […]
