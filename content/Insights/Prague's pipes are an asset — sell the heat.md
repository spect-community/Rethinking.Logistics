## From the transcript

> The good news is, you have a district heating network in place. So what that means is you have the pipes in the ground to distribute that heat. […] you've got a high share of district heating in Northern Europe and Eastern Europe, and then in Western Europe and Southern Europe, it's not very common. […] If you have one system in Prague, then that's good. That means if you locate data centres in certain parts of the city, not too far from the pipes in the ground, then that means you can effectively do that scheme where you can plug your data centre, collect its heat with a heat pump in various systems, and then effectively re-inject it into the pipes […] I could build a pipe in the ground to connect my data center to the district heating system of Prague and then essentially sell my heat to Prague. […] So that's what's happening, for example, very commonly in Finland or in Denmark or in Sweden, where the district heating is quite developed.
