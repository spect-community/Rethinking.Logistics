## Context

In a follow-up to a student's question about converting abandoned buildings into data centres, the questioner suggested that a data centre's waste heat could make a poorly insulated existing envelope less of a problem. Paul Astle (Ramboll) agrees that reusing waste heat helps, but explains why it cannot remove the cooling load.

## From the transcript

> If you can use that waste heat for benefit and that waste heat displaces someone else's heating, then obviously that's beneficial, however data centers have to be designed for normally for 100, well pretty much for 100% uptime, which means that you have to be able to deal with that heat in the hottest day in summer as well as the coldest day in winter, which means you have to have a lot of chillers and things to get rid of that heat and that's where the energy consumption comes from. So definitely if you can reuse the heat it would bring down the impact, but I don't think it would eliminate it […] But data centers are basically heaters. They are giant heaters and we spend a massive amount of energy in creating the heat and then we spend almost the same amount of energy in cooling it afterwards.
