## Context

František Novotný (CREA-TURA) presents the multi-storey food (cake, biscuit and snack-bar) factory in the centre of Frýdek-Místek, built on a former textile-factory brownfield next to housing, a school and the river. Explaining the landscape of the site, he says that food-hygiene rules leave almost no room for biodiversity measures, which is why such factories look sterile.

## From the transcript

> Once we are in the food produce manufacturing, there are very strict limitations for insects, rodents and everything. That's something which makes those factories look like frigid because you definitely cannot introduce any other biodiversity to this site, not by this law we are using right now. Maybe it will change in the future, but right now […] once you are attending this typology, you are asked by the client: do not put there anything that makes it better for the biodiversity, because once you do it, they have to poison everything on the outside, so the biologic life doesn't go inside the factory […]. It's something that is very highly controlled. […] You need to meet […] not only Europe, not only Czech standards, but also like international standards for the hygiene of the food produced, and that goes actually contrary to what you might as architects want to be interested in, for example like green.
