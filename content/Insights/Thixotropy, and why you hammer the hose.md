## Context

Near the end of the Q&A, after a remark about the hammer being a handy tool in printing, Vele (CTU Prague / Scoolpt) explains why crews hammer the hose and how the printing mix flows. The transcript says there are about five minutes to resume printing after a stop and that material can set in the hose even during the shortest pause; it does not say that a pause of five minutes is enough to stiffen it.

## From the transcript

> The hose is like, you know, it's very, very long from the pump to the nozzle and when you stop printing, you have like 5 minutes to start again printing. But even if you have the lowest time from one piece to another piece of printing, the material can get solid inside the hose. So that's why you need to hammer […] the concrete somehow starts flowing again inside the hose. Interesting material this one, because it's not the regular concrete. This one is very thixotropic. […] when you are giving some energy to the material, it's liquid, and when you are not giving the energy, it's solid. So imagine that you have this concrete and you create like a pyramid out of it on a table. It's very solid. […] But if you start wiggling with the table, it becomes like a honey and it just flows away. […] inside the pump, inside the hose, inside the nozzle, there are vibrations. So it's liquid, and when it flows outside and there is no vibrations, it becomes solid. So it can like hold its shape before the chemical reaction kicks in. And that's the reason why also we need [a] hammer.
