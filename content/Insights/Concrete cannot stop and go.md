## Context

Discussing the minimal-surface bridge study, Vele (CTU Prague / Scoolpt) explains why such structures have to be printed in sections and why their finish is rough.

## From the transcript

> When you have these structures, you do need to 3D print them in steps because the print path is not continuous. And most of the 3D printing technologies, they cannot do the amazing thing that's done in plastic, that you print one layer and then you retract, move somewhere else, print that layer somewhere else and then retract and move. So this stop and go is not as automatized in concrete as in plastic. […] And still you can see that the finishes are kind of ugly and that's because of the technology, because you have this pump, you have a hose, there is a big pressure and even when you stop pumping, there is still pressure build up inside the hose and it's still flowing out. And also when you start printing and you press on the pump, the pressure still needs to build up inside the hose before it actually really starts flowing out.
