## Edition 1.1

Edition 1.0 documented the **design level**: the spatial challenges of logistics architecture and how the student projects measured them. Edition 1.1 adds the **system level** — how logistics buildings are financed, leased, certified and valued — so that the argument of the studio's final report can be traced to notes: good logistics architecture is held back less by design than by the system around it.

**New note types**

- **Problem statements** — 7 system-level problems, from the split incentive between owner and tenant to certificates met on paper. Each links to its metrics, trade-offs and evidence. See [[Problem statements]].
- **Trade-offs** — 17 recurring conflicts between design aims, synthesised from the trade-off sections of the case studies and from publications. See [[Trade-offs]].

**New content**

- An investor view of performance: financial metrics such as [[Net operating income]], [[Capitalisation rate]], [[Green rent premium]] and [[Clear height]], drawn from the ULI report *Sustainability Unboxed* (2025) and an industry KPI glossary.
- Evidence on labour, leases and certification: [[Labour-to-rent cost ratio]], [[Staff turnover and replacement cost]], [[Oversizing ratio]] and statements from industry research, peer-reviewed studies and the studio's industry partners.
- A new challenge, [[Designing for an unknown tenant]], and a *Common misreading* section on challenges — how a requirement can be met on paper without fulfilling its intention, and what to do instead.
- Gaps named in the notes are now collected on the [[Evidence gaps]] page.

Every new statement is labelled with its source and is open for expert review; see [[Evidence status]].

## Edition 1.0

The first complete edition: ambitions, challenges, metrics, tools, case studies, reference projects, domains, strategies and insights from the *Rethinking.Logistics* studio at ARCHIP Prague (2026), each statement with its evidence status.
